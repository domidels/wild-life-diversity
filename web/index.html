After the fire: what changed in what people observe?

Satellites record where fire burned. Naturalists record which species they see. Crossing the two, before and after major wildfires, we compare burned land with nearby unburned land to separate the effect of fire from everything else.

06Did any species' observations move?

Mean latitude of each species' observations, after minus before. Whiskers show ±2 standard errors; filled dots are shifts larger than that. Within one region this often reflects where observers went — a lead to check, not a conclusion.

07How to read this

Design: BACI

Before-After-Control-Impact. For each metric,
effect = (burned after ÷ before) ÷ (control after ÷ before).
Dividing by the control cancels changes that happened everywhere: more app users, a rainy year, a lockdown.

Limits

  • GBIF records presence, not abundance: these are observations, not populations.
  • Observers avoid burned land for a while, then come back to look — effort itself reacts to fire.
  • Control cells are not random: they may differ in habitat or access.
  • “New to the region” is sensitive to effort and to taxonomic changes.

Pipeline

  1. A monthly Databricks job pulls GBIF occurrences and NASA FIRMS detections into a Unity Catalog volume.
  2. dbt builds bronze → silver → gold tables: H3 grid, burn zones per fire, BACI, rarefaction — tested at every layer.
  3. GitHub Actions exports the gold tables to JSON and publishes this page.
